01

Your first session.

What to expect from booking through follow-up.

What does setup look like when you arrive?

I'll arrive about ten minutes early to bring in the table and set up. You'll need a clear floor space (roughly 7'×4' with room to walk around it) such as a bedroom, a spare room, or a tidy corner of the living room. The setup itself takes about ten minutes. Once we're going, treatment time is the time you booked, never less.

Will you talk to me during the massage?

Only as much as you want. We'll check in at the start about pressure, focus areas, and music. After that, you're welcome to drift off in silence or chat the whole way through. Both are completely fine. I'll always speak up before changing pressure significantly or moving to a sensitive area.

What should I wear?

You'll undress to your level of comfort and lie under a sheet and blanket. Most clients prefer underwear on; some undress fully and stay under the linens; some keep on shorts and a sports bra. All of those are fine. Only the body part being worked on is uncovered at any time.

02

Your space.

A few requirements that help mobile work feel like clinical work.

What kind of space do I need to provide?

A private room, large enough for the table (6' × 3') with a foot of clearance on all sides. The room should be quiet for the duration of the appointment with kids and pets settled elsewhere and electronics turned off.

What if I have a pet?

I love animals, but I do need pets settled in another room or outside for the duration of the session. It's a clinical safety issue (sterile field) and a focus issue. If your pet is anxious about being shut out, let me know in advance and we can plan around it.

Can someone else be in the room?

For a youth/child massage, yes, the parent is welcome to remain present. For everything else, no. Confidentiality and privacy require the room be just the two of us. A partner or roommate elsewhere in the home is fine.

03

Booking & payment.

How appointments are scheduled, paid, and confirmed.

How do I book?

Scheduling runs through Jane, the practice-management platform used by most Alberta RMTs. The Book button anywhere on this site opens my Jane page, where you can see real availability, pick a session length, and confirm in a couple of minutes. Email and phone work too if you'd rather not book online. A credit card on file holds the booking; you're not charged until after the session.

What forms do I fill out, and when?

Everything happens in Jane before your first appointment, so no paperwork eats into treatment time. When you book, Jane emails you a confirmation with a secure link to two things: a health-history intake form and a consent form. The health history covers current concerns, past injuries and surgeries, medications, and anything that changes how I work, and takes about five minutes. The consent form explains scope of practice, what areas may be treated, your right to modify or stop treatment at any point, and how your information is stored.

Both are stored in Jane's encrypted, Canadian-hosted records, so returning clients only confirm what has changed rather than starting over. If you'd prefer paper, tell me when you book and I'll bring printed copies to complete before we begin, which adds about ten minutes to the visit.

How do I pay?

Tap-to-pay on a portable terminal at the end of the session, e-transfer, or in most cases direct billing to your insurance. Receipts are emailed automatically.

Do you offer gift cards?

Yes. Gift cards are available in any amount and can be sent by email or printed. Spend $500 or more in one purchase and the recipient gets an extra 10% loaded on top.

04

Cancellation & no-show.

A clear policy that protects everyone's time.

What's the cancellation policy?

Twenty-four hours, please. Cancellations or reschedules made less than 24 hours before the appointment are billed a $75 fee to the card on file. No-shows are billed at full price. Both fees can be waived once for genuine emergencies.

What if I'm sick?

Please reschedule, even at the last minute. Illness, including colds, fevers, or anything contagious, always waives the cancellation fee. I'd rather see you when you're well than treat through something that should be in bed.

What if you cancel on me?

If I cancel less than 24 hours out (it happens: illness, a T1D issue, a vehicle problem), I'll work with you to find a replacement time and offer 20% off the rebooked session by way of apology.

05

Insurance & direct billing.

Most major Canadian insurers, submitted on your behalf.

Which insurers do you direct-bill?

Alberta Blue Cross, TELUS Health (formerly Greenshield), Sun Life / Lumino, Canada Life, Manulife, and most plans accessible through ProviderConnect. I'm an MTAA-registered RMT, which is the credential nearly all Canadian insurers require.

Will I owe anything at the end of the session?

It depends on your plan, but the short version: I check your coverage and submit the claim live at the end of the session, then charge only any remaining balance. Most clients with massage benefits walk away owing zero.

What might leave a small balance: a per-visit cap below my session rate (e.g. some plans cover $80 / visit; you'd owe the difference) or an annual benefit that's been used up.

If you want zero surprises, ask me to pre-check your coverage before your appointment. With your written consent I can usually verify the same day you book.

What if my plan doesn't direct-bill?

You'll get a fully itemized receipt with my registration number and association. Submit it to your provider for reimbursement. Receipts are emailed within an hour of the session.

Is GST included in the listed prices?

Yes. The prices you see on the services page are the all-in amounts. GST (5%) is already included, so what you see is what you pay. Most insurance plans reimburse the full amount.

06

A note on Type 1 Diabetes.

I live with T1D and want to be transparent about how that shows up at work.

How does T1D affect a session?

Almost never visibly. Massage can be rigorous exercise for the therapist, and like any exercise it can dramatically increase insulin sensitivity. Because my pancreas can't dial insulin output up or down on its own, I manage that with a continuous glucose monitor (CGM) and fast-acting carbohydrates I keep close at hand. Before every session I check my numbers, eat appropriately, and dose insulin so I'm starting in a stable range.

Will I notice anything during my session?

Probably not. In rare cases, usually after a string of busy bookings, my CGM may quietly alert and I'll need to pause for sixty seconds to eat something. If that happens, I'll let you know what's going on, take a quick break, and pick right back up. The rest of your time is reserved and uncompromised.

Why mention it at all?

Two reasons. First, I'd rather you know what's happening than wonder. Second, T1D affects roughly one in three hundred Canadians, and the more it's normalized in professional spaces, the better. If you have T1D yourself and want to chat, I'm always glad to.

What's an emergency plan look like?

Vanishingly unlikely to be needed, but worth saying clearly: if I were ever to become unresponsive, the fastest action is to call 911 and tell them I'm a Type 1 Diabetic experiencing severe hypoglycemia. My emergency contact info is in a small folio in my supply bag.

07

Safety & boundaries.

A short, firm section.

Is it safe to have a stranger come to my home?

It's a fair question to ask, especially as a first-time client. A few practical points:

I'm a Registered Massage Therapist in good standing with the MTAA, verifiable through their public registry. I carry professional liability and commercial general liability insurance, with documents available on request. I run a registered Alberta business, hold a City of Edmonton licence, and operate under my own name, not a brand alias.

On the practical safety side: I'm fully trained in trauma-informed practice, you set the boundaries of the session, and you can pause or end any treatment at any moment with no explanation needed. If anything ever feels off, whether it's pressure, tone, or the environment, saying so is enough.

Many clients also like to know I share my live location with a trusted contact during every appointment. It's a small thing but it cuts both ways: safer for you to know I'm tracked, safer for me too.

What's your scope of practice?

I'm trained to assess and treat soft-tissue and joint dysfunction within the scope of an Alberta Registered Massage Therapist. I do not diagnose conditions, prescribe medications, or perform medical adjustments. For those, I'll refer you to the right professional. I'm happy to coordinate with your physiotherapist, chiropractor, or family doctor if you'd like.

Do you treat pregnancy?

Yes, after the first trimester, with your provider's clearance. I use side-lying and pillow-supported positions; the table never compresses the abdomen.

Is the practice trauma-informed?

Yes. Consent is checked at every transition. Pressure is collaborative. You always retain the right to pause, adjust, or end a session without question. Mobile work happens in your space, which means your control over the environment is even greater than at a clinic, and I treat that as a feature, not a complication.

What if something feels off?

Tell me, during the session if you can, after if it's easier. I take feedback seriously, will adjust on the spot, and will never make you justify a request to change pressure, position, or focus.

Still have a question?

Email or text. I respond within one business day.
