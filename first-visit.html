Your First Visit

What to expect.

A clear, calm walk-through of the experience, from the moment you book through the day after. Mobile massage is unfamiliar to most people the first time. Here's exactly how it goes.

Step 01

Before your session.

Booking runs through Jane. You pick a session length and time from real availability, and Jane emails a confirmation with a secure link to your intake.

Health history and consent

Two forms are waiting in that link, and both are completed online before I arrive so nothing eats into treatment time. The health history takes about five minutes and covers medications, injuries and surgeries, current concerns, and the areas you want focus on. The consent form sets out scope of practice, which areas may be treated, and your right to modify or stop treatment at any point. Records are stored in Jane's encrypted Canadian-hosted system, so returning visits only need a quick confirmation of what has changed.

If you'd rather do paper, say so when you book and I'll bring printed copies. Budget an extra ten minutes for the first visit if so.

Preparing your space

  • A clear floor area roughly 7' × 4', bedroom, spare room, or a tidied corner of the living room all work.
  • Settle pets and kids elsewhere for the duration. It's a clinical safety issue and a focus issue.
  • Comfortable room temperature: slightly warmer than usual is best. An extra blanket nearby is helpful if you run cold.
  • Quiet, if you can. Phone notifications off, TVs off, doors that can close.

A few practical notes

You don't need to provide anything else, I bring the table, linens, oil, heater, music. A short shower beforehand is welcome but not required. Eat lightly an hour or so before.

Step 02

When I arrive.

I aim to arrive ten minutes early. I'll knock or buzz quietly, introduce myself, and ask where you'd like the table set up. Setup takes about ten minutes and includes the table, fresh linens, a portable speaker, and a small side table for my notes and any tools.

Once everything's ready, we'll sit down for a brief intake, five to ten minutes. I'll review your form, ask about your day, and confirm what you'd like out of the session: relaxation, focus on a specific area, or both. This is also when we decide on music, lighting, and how chatty (or quiet) you'd like the session to be.

Step 03

During treatment.

I'll step out while you undress to your level of comfort and get under the linens. Most clients undress fully and stay under the sheet; some prefer underwear; some keep on shorts. All of those are fine. Only the body part being worked is uncovered at any time.

What the session feels like

I'll check in about pressure as we start each new area, and adapt continuously based on your feedback. Most sessions include some combination of Swedish (relaxation), trigger point (focused tension), myofascial work (slow, broad strokes for connective tissue), and stretching.

If you booked the signature scalp, face, and neck work, that typically wraps the last 20 minutes, when your body is most relaxed and the parasympathetic shift is deepest. It's the part most clients tell me they remember.

Your control over the session

You can pause, ask me to lighten or deepen pressure, request more or less work on an area, or end the session, at any moment, without explanation. Trauma-informed practice means consent is checked at every transition, and you never have to justify a preference.

Step 04

Payment & billing.

After the session, I'll step out while you get dressed. We'll handle payment together once you're up and ready, no rush, no awkward upsell.

If you have benefits, I'll submit your insurance claim live on a tablet, then charge you only any remaining balance. Most clients owe nothing. If your plan doesn't direct-bill, I'll email you a fully itemized receipt within an hour so you can submit it yourself.

Accepted: tap card, e-transfer, cash. GST is included in the listed prices; most insurance plans cover the full amount.

Step 05

After the session.

Two or three home-care suggestions will be in your inbox by the next morning, stretches, mobility cues, or rest recommendations based on what we worked on. They're optional, not homework.

It's common to feel a little tender the next day, especially after deeper work. Drink water, move gently, and let me know if anything surprises you, I want feedback, both good and constructive.

If you'd like to rebook, you can do so online any time, or just reply to the receipt email and I'll set something up.

A few common concerns

What if…

My place is small and I feel self-conscious about it.

Honestly, this comes up a lot. I've worked in studio apartments, basement suites, and homes mid-renovation. I'm not here to judge your space, only to do good work in it. As long as the floor is clear and the room is private, we're set.

I've never had a massage before.

Welcome. Tell me at intake that this is your first, and I'll adjust pressure conservatively and explain each transition. 75 minutes is a good first booking, long enough to be worth the visit, short enough not to overwhelm.

I'm worried about feeling too vulnerable having someone in my home.

A reasonable thing to think about. The Safety & Boundaries FAQ covers verification, insurance, location-sharing, and trauma-informed practice in detail. You can also have a friend or partner home elsewhere in the place.

I might fall asleep.

Many people do. It's a genuine compliment, and I won't take it personally. I'll wake you gently a few minutes before the end so you have time to come back into the room.

Ready to book your first session?

A 75-minute booking is a good starting length for most new clients.
