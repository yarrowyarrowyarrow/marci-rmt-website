What I Treat

Neck & shoulder pain.

What it is

Persistent aching through the neck, upper trapezius, and between the shoulder blades, most often from long hours in a sustained forward posture rather than from a single injury.

What it feels like

A heavy, gripping ache across the tops of the shoulders that stiffens after sitting and can refer up into the base of the skull or down the arm.

Massage considerations

How I approach it.

  • Treatment covers the upper trapezius, levator scapulae, scalenes, and the muscles between the shoulder blades, with attention to how the shoulder actually moves rather than just where it hurts.
  • Positioning matters more than pressure here, so side-lying and supported prone work are used freely.
  • If symptoms travel down the arm with numbness or weakness, that gets assessed before any deep work.
What the evidence says

The research, briefly.

  • Massage therapy shows moderate short-term benefit for neck pain in Cochrane and systematic review evidence, particularly when combined with movement.
  • Trials consistently find the largest gains where treatment is paired with home exercise rather than delivered alone.
  • Benefit is measured in weeks, so a short series usually outperforms a single visit.
  1. Patel, K. C., et al. (2012). Massage for mechanical neck disorders. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, (9), CD004871.
  2. Cheng, Y. H., & Huang, G. C. (2014). Efficacy of massage therapy on pain and dysfunction in patients with neck pain. Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative Medicine, 2014, 204360.

Educational, not diagnostic. If symptoms are new, severe, or changing, please see a physician first.
