What I Treat

Repetitive strain.

What it is

Repetitive strain describes tissue irritation from sustained low-load use, most often in the forearm, wrist, and hand of people who type, lift, or grip all day.

What it feels like

Burning or aching through the forearm, weakness in grip, and tingling in the fingers that is often worse at night or first thing in the morning.

Massage considerations

How I approach it.

  • Treatment covers the forearm flexors and extensors, the elbow attachments, and the shoulder girdle above them, since the whole chain contributes.
  • Nerve-entrapment presentations such as carpal tunnel need gentler, more specific handling than tendon irritation does.
  • Sessions include load management advice, because the exposure that caused it will undo the work if nothing changes.
What the evidence says

The research, briefly.

  • Trials of soft-tissue treatment for lateral elbow and forearm conditions report short-term reductions in pain and improvements in grip strength.
  • Massage and manual therapy for carpal tunnel syndrome show benefit for symptom severity and function in several randomised studies.
  • Outcomes are best where treatment is paired with ergonomic and workload changes.
  1. Nourbakhsh, M. R., & Fearon, F. J. (2008). The effect of oscillating-energy manual therapy on lateral epicondylitis. Journal of Hand Therapy, 21(1), 4–13.
  2. Elliott, R., & Burkett, B. (2013). Massage therapy as an effective treatment for carpal tunnel syndrome. Journal of Bodywork and Movement Therapies, 17(3), 332–338.

Educational, not diagnostic. If symptoms are new, severe, or changing, please see a physician first.
