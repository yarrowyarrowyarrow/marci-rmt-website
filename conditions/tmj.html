What I Treat

TMJ & jaw tension.

What it is

Temporomandibular discomfort involves the jaw joint and the muscles that move it, frequently linked to clenching, grinding, or sustained daytime bracing.

What it feels like

Aching or fatigue through the jaw and temples, clicking on opening, tenderness when chewing, and often headaches that sit behind the eyes.

Massage considerations

How I approach it.

  • External work through the masseter, temporalis, and pterygoid region is effective for most people, and intraoral technique is only used with explicit consent.
  • Sessions here are short-duration and precise, since jaw muscles respond poorly to prolonged heavy pressure.
  • Persistent locking, or an inability to open, is a dental or medical referral first.
What the evidence says

The research, briefly.

  • Randomised trials find manual therapy to the masticatory muscles reduces pain and improves mouth opening in temporomandibular disorders.
  • Systematic reviews support soft-tissue and manual approaches as reasonable conservative care alongside dental management.
  • Outcomes improve when treatment is combined with awareness of daytime clenching habits.
  1. Armijo-Olivo, S., et al. (2016). Effectiveness of manual therapy and therapeutic exercise for temporomandibular disorders: systematic review and meta-analysis. Physical Therapy, 96(1), 9–25.
  2. Calixtre, L. B., et al. (2015). Manual therapy for the management of pain and limited range of motion in subjects with signs of temporomandibular joint disorders. Journal of Oral Rehabilitation, 42(11), 847–861.

Educational, not diagnostic. If symptoms are new, severe, or changing, please see a physician first.
